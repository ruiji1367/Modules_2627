Software quality

Software quality is the degree to which a software product meets its gathered requirements.

Application A

Many features, but fails important requirements.

Application B

Fewer features, but correctly meets the agreed requirements.

Which application better satisfies the stated requirements?

Quality also means fitness for intended use. Meeting an incomplete or incorrect set of requirements is not enough.

Software quality assurance (SQA)

Planned activities that give confidence that the development process is appropriate and produces software of suitable quality.

What is a prototype?

An early representation of a software product used to explore ideas, communicate design decisions, or evaluate functionality. It may lack complete business logic.

Why prototype before building?

Team 1

Builds everything, then users say ordering is confusing.

Requirements→Implementation→Users confused

Team 2

Shows a prototype first.

Requirements→Prototype→Feedback→Revision→Implementation
  • Users evaluate design and behavior
  • Requirements and functionality become clearer
  • Design gaps appear before implementation
  • May reduce avoidable rework, cost, and time

Prototyping may help, but it does not guarantee lower cost in every project.

Two prototyping paradigms

Use throwaway when

  • Requirements are unclear
  • Exploring early design ideas
  • Comparing interface options

Use evolutionary when

  • Requirements are reasonably understood
  • Iterative development is planned
  • The architecture can support refinement

Case study: campus food ordering

The university wants students to browse food, order, and view order status. The team is unsure about the best ordering flow. Decide: low or high fidelity? Throwaway or evolutionary? What do users evaluate first? What happens after feedback?

  • Start low-fidelity (paper or wireframe): the flow is the uncertainty.
  • Use throwaway prototyping to compare flows without committing.
  • Users first evaluate navigation and the ordering steps.
  • Revise using feedback, then move to higher fidelity and implementation.

Other approaches can be valid if justified.
